# Afterword: How 'Unmasking Div Grad Curl' Came to Be

I would like to extend my deepest respect to you, the reader, who has completed this long journey.

As you close this book, allow me to share a bit of behind-the-scenes story about how this peculiar and somewhat bold-structured 'Unmasking Div Grad Curl' came into existence.

The whole thing started with my own bitter experience. Like many students of physics, I too nearly drowned in the quagmire of "vector calculus." Confronted with $\mathrm{rot}$ and $\mathrm{div}$ in cylindrical or spherical coordinates, the question "why does it take this form?" was silenced by the pressure to "just memorize the formulas." I knew that differential forms were the silver bullet, but the axiomatic approach of mathematics books was too abstract for me as a beginner.

"Could there be a safe and powerful 'shortcut' to escape the old regime's quagmire?" — As I pondered this, the first breakthrough came to me. This is a personal story, but at the time I was deeply immersed in studying quantum mechanics and had become accustomed to handling Dirac's "bra-ket notation." The feeling of laying state vectors (column vectors) on their sides as dual vectors (row vectors) and elegantly computing with matrix multiplication — that sensation. It suddenly occurred to me: what if I could directly apply this intuition from quantum mechanics onto the classical $dx, dy, dz$ in three-dimensional space? By redefining the $dx$ at the tail of the integral sign as a "row vector (matrix) that extracts components from a displacement vector," the abstract concept of "a function that eats vectors (1-form)" could be instantaneously transformed into "matrix multiplication" that everyone knows.

But this alone was still not enough. The second breakthrough came from W. L. Burke's powerful pedagogical philosophy: "put the metric later and later into the course." I made a decision: I would turn this profound philosophy into an "entertainment of table of contents structure" that draws readers in.

From Chapter 1 through Chapter 4, we did not formally define the concept of metric. We pressed forward with the naive definitions of length and area that we have used since elementary school, in real space $(x,y,z)$. A mathematician might frown and say "you need a metric for area," but as long as the Pythagorean theorem holds in $xyz$ Cartesian coordinates, that is sufficient. The metric $g = J^T J$ truly becomes necessary when we generalize to coordinate systems with distorted scales like parameter spaces — we eruptively revealed that fact through matrix calculations in Chapter 5.

Then came the opening of Chapter 5. With "the author is getting tired of this too," we cut off the excuses to mathematicians and formally defined the inner product. At the same time, we revealed the true identity of the metric $g = J^T J$, and positioned the Hodge star $\ast$ as a "type-conversion adapter connecting differential forms to vector calculus." Through this discovery-based construction, the setup was designed to make readers thoroughly convinced, down to the marrow of their bones, of the truth that "the gradient ($d$) does not depend on the metric, but the curl and divergence ($\ast d$ or $\ast d \ast$) do depend on the metric."

This book is a "survival guide" written for my past self, who had stumbled in vector calculus. Now that you know the limitations of the tool — that "the Hodge star $\ast$ depends on the distortion of space (the metric)" — you have already opened the door to the next world.

Here is the last thing I want to share with you, who has mastered this book. This book achieved success by rigorously separating "metric (length, angle)" and "geometry (topology)." However, at the frontiers of modern physics exists the ultimate world of "Geometric Algebra" as advocated by C. Doran and A. Lasenby and others. There, the inner product and outer product that we struggled to separate are completely integrated from the start as a single "geometric product." Precisely because you have suffered through and understood the meaning of this separation, when you encounter that "integration" paradigm next, you will be able to understand its true brilliance and beauty.

As the author, I sincerely hope that 'Unmasking Div Grad Curl' will serve as a robust stepping stone for your journey in physics, enabling you to fly higher and farther.
