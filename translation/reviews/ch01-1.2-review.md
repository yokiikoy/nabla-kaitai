## Important Translation Decisions

### 1. `df` as an operator

The translation preserves the central contract: `df` itself is not a numerical change. The numerical change is read as `df(v)`, namely the linear or principal part of the change.

### 2. `dy`, `dz`

The English version applies the same contract as for `dx`: standalone `dy` and `dz` are operators / row vectors / `$1$-forms`; scalar widths are written as `Delta y`, `Delta z`, or as evaluations such as `dy(v)` and `dz(v)`.

### 3. Substitution and pullback

The substitution formula `dx = gamma'(t) dt` is presented in the standard calculus way first, then reread as a rebuilding of measuring devices. The note clarifies that, in the later Chapter 4 language, the safer expression is `gamma^*(dx)=gamma'(t) dt`.

### 4. Three-dimensional extension

The translation keeps the pedagogical decision to embed one-variable and two-variable cases into the three-dimensional matrix framework.
